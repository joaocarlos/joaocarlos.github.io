## Abstract

IoT-based emergency management systems are a growing trend in smart city development, designed to maintain fast alarm responses even under heavy load or adversarial conditions. Because of the nature of these systems, emergency alarms have the highest priority and must be processed as quickly as possible. However, existing priority-based schedulers often rank messages by geographic zone importance, creating semantic–geographic priority inversion in which emergency alarms from low-priority zones can be delayed by routine traffic from high-priority regions. In addition, typical FIFO tie-breaking within a given priority class can enable device monopolization and reduce observability during concurrent emergencies. To address these limitations, we introduce TRIAGE/4 (Tiered Resource Allocation for IoT Alarm and Geographic-priority Emergency), a priority-based queuing approach that unconditionally promotes emergency alarms while also respecting the spatial priorities of transmitting source sensors. To that end, we define four operational bands to support the proposed concept of “semantic override”, which dynamically adjusts scheduling behavior according to the incoming transmission flow. We evaluate TRIAGE/4 via discrete-event simulation against five scheduling baselines (strict geographic priority, FIFO, weighted fair queueing, deficit round-robin, and token-bucket priority) across inversion, monopolization, multi-zone emergencies, overload, and stress sweeps. In the simulated experiments, TRIAGE/4 reduced alarm mean latency by up to 94.4% and improved device latency fairness by 29.4% relative to strict geographic priority. Under alarm message floods, limiting each source to its own emission rate contained 93.0% of the abnormal traffic without shedding any genuine alarms, holding the alarm 95th-percentile latency at 0.150 s compared with 7.270 s under strict geographic priority. A prototype MQTT broker deployed on a Raspberry Pi 5 confirms improved alarm responsiveness on real hardware, with a per-message scheduling cost of 15 $\mu $ s at the median and 50 $\mu $ s at the 99th percentile. This would directly benefit priority-centered IoT emergency systems based on multi-sensor detection units.
